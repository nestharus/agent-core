# Workspace storage and cleanup

Keep active work usable and preserve unfinished source and required evidence while reclaiming disposable data. Apply the user's current authorization and the actual owners' preservation decisions; age, filenames and ticket status alone do not establish deletion safety.

## Workspaces and review outputs

- Use the [canonical project layout](project-layout.md). Look for an existing suitable checkout or released worktree before creating another. Create additional worktrees for immediate parallel work that requires isolation; read-only review does not automatically require a checkout or repository copy.
- Reuse an idle worktree only after its owner releases it and its source, untracked files, ignored inputs and required results are conserved. Use supported Git worktree operations for moves and removals, and update the inventory and actual consumers of the old path. A tool that only reacquires the same owner/task/pin binding is not a cross-task reuse pool.
- For ordinary worktree removal under this user's cleanup rule, require a clean checkout, at least 24 hours without changes, and the exact local head matching the selected remote branch head, with no unpublished required commits. Record that binding. Preserve dirty, unpublished, reserved, active or otherwise unqualified work. Paused work can be stored remotely within existing publication authority and referenced in the work inventory before retiring its local checkout.
- Give each review or investigation one approved task area and one attempt directory per actual invocation. At completion, retain the source/ref and findings needed to understand the result, identify the disposable prompt/build material, and retire its checkout when eligible. Avoid copying prompts and entire repositories into multiple unrelated folders. Markdown under planning can be a durable database or behavior-authoritative input; do not classify it as a disposable prompt by extension or heading.

## Artifacts and conservation

- Classify generated targets, dependencies, temporary files and agent prompts by their creating purpose and current use. For this user's cleanup, 24 hours is the minimum age for eligible temporary artifacts. Preserve active or reserved inputs and declared holds, including those inside `.tmp` folders or Git checkouts. Coordinate uncertain deletion and conservation needs with the actual owners; proceed on existing exact grants without a new unrelated Root/Main/provider/review approval queue.
- Preserve the source, WIP, revisions, private inputs and historical results that retain the work's meaning. Tested executables, coverage/report objects, profiles and failed or partial results can be oracles even when stored beside compiler caches. A later rebuild is not automatically the tested image. Discard acceleration caches only within their actual release; do not promise exact-byte reconstruction or infer test success or task closure.
- Before each destructive effect, revalidate the admitted path and identity, age and unchanged metadata. Keep changed, unavailable, linked or unknown members unless their exact case has separately qualified. Exclude quarantined captures and every exposing alias, fragment, derivative and mixed stream before enumeration, metadata, body/hash acquisition, copying or deletion. Do not follow symlinks into unselected areas.
- Record actual effects and partial failures, distinguish pending intents from completed removals, and close completed authorization guards. Resume only a deliberately selected remaining operation; never replay a completed deletion. Join intentional discards into the restore inventory so an older remote copy does not silently reintroduce them. Whole-folder retirement requires its own complete conservation and current-use determination.

## Bound growth and inspection

- Start with filesystem capacity and known heavy directories or existing metadata inventories. Avoid repeated whole-drive scans. Count bind mounts and hard-linked data with their actual identity; allocated entry totals are not unique physical reclaim. Measure available space and inodes after cleanup, retaining the observation window's limits.
- Distinguish retention from access restrictions during size inventory. An approved area or `KEEP` label records topology or a deletion hold; it does not automatically exclude the folder from byte accounting. Apply explicit opaque, no-metadata and no-traversal restrictions before access. Estimate allocation with filesystem metadata, without opening credential, session, database or recovery contents. Report excluded and unmeasured scopes explicitly; a size result grants no deletion authority.
- Continue expensive inventories from a bounded checkpoint instead of repeatedly scanning their completed portions. Preserve actual child failures, access gaps and changed-directory identities. An empty remaining frontier establishes coverage only within the recorded exclusions and errors; totals from different intervals do not establish a stable whole-drive census.
- For one-off direct Codex investigations or reviews that do not need continuation, request `--ephemeral` and bounded capture, such as the [launcher's](../tools/direct-codex-child/README.md) `--log-max-bytes 33554432`. Preserve incomplete/truncated capture status. Keep persistent roots and required recovery material under their actual retention duties; this is not permission to purge client stores.
- Use absolute paths for task inventories and other prompt resources. The launcher snapshots the prompt into an attempt directory, so a resource described as "beside this prompt" may not be there. Keep the original resource path explicit instead of searching the drive for a missing relative reference.
- The legacy `ai-space-cleanup` and `ai-dot-tmp-cleanup` bulk scan/apply paths are not qualified for the current preservation boundaries: they inspect native/profile or process data and traverse broad areas. Do not run them for this cleanup. Use an explicitly scoped operation that honors current exclusions; do not inspect credentials, process environments, client/session stores or raw recovery captures to guess which data is disposable.

These instructions select operating practices. They do not establish that a reuse pool, automatic retention scheduler or complete drive-wide cleanup has been deployed.
